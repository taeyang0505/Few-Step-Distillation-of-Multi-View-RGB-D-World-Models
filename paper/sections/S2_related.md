# 2 Related Work

**Video generation for robot world models.** Video diffusion models have been used as visual world models for manipulation: the model predicts future frames from the current observation, and actions are read off the predicted frames. Dreamitate (Liang et al., 2024) uses a fine-tuned video generation model as a visuomotor policy, whereas Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) and DP3 (Ze et al., 2024) predict actions directly from images or point clouds. Geo4D (Liu et al., 2025) extends Stable Video Diffusion (Blattmann et al., 2023) to generate RGB and pointmaps jointly for two views, with a cross-view alignment loss that places both pointmaps in the reference camera frame; it samples with the EDM scheme (Karras et al., 2022) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022). On the PlaceAppleFromBowlIntoBin task in the LBM simulator (Toyota Research Institute, 2025) it reaches a success rate of 0.53 against 0.10 for Dreamitate and 0.00 for DP and DP3; its stated limitation is inference time, about 30 s per 10 frames on an RTX 4090. DreamDojo (Gao et al., 2026) and RoboWorld (Jeon et al., 2026) accelerate robot world models to 10.8 and 15.3 FPS, but neither evaluates multi-view geometry.

**Few-step diffusion and distillation.** Progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022) halves the number of sampling steps repeatedly, and consistency models (Song et al., 2023) map any point on the probability-flow ODE to its endpoint. Distribution matching distillation (DMD; Yin et al., 2024a) trains a one-step generator by matching the score of its output distribution to the teacher's; DMD2 (Yin et al., 2024b) removes the regression term and adds multi-step backward simulation. For video, CausVid (Yin et al., 2025), Self-Forcing (Huang et al., 2025) and Causal Forcing (Zhu et al., 2026) distill bidirectional teachers into few-step autoregressive students; Seaweed-APT (Lin et al., 2025) adds adversarial post-training toward one step; MAGI-1 (Sand.ai, 2025) and StreamDiT (Kodaira et al., 2025) target streaming generation. These works target RGB video quality and speed; none of them evaluates depth or cross-view consistency, the quantity a geometry-aware world model exists to provide. We apply DMD with DMD2-style backward simulation to Geo4D and evaluate depth error, cross-view consistency, LPIPS, sharpness and seed diversity with paired tests (Wilcoxon, 1945).

**Training-free acceleration and evaluation of geometry.** Orthogonal to distillation, TeaCache (F. Liu et al., 2025) and MagCache (Ma et al., 2025) reuse features across adjacent denoising steps, and SVDQuant (Li et al., 2025) quantizes the diffusion model. Caching gains shrink once few steps remain to share; quantization still applies after distillation and is left as future work. Evaluating few-step samplers is itself delicate. Blau & Michaeli (2018) show that distortion metrics such as PSNR favor the conditional mean, so a blurred sample can score higher than a sharp one, while LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018) penalizes the blur. We observe the same effect in RGB-D: PSNR rises as the step count falls while diversity, LPIPS and cross-view geometry degrade, which motivates the metric set in Section 4.
