# Abstract

Multi-view RGB-D world models such as Geo4D (Liu et al., 2025) let a robot plan in 3D, but their iterative samplers are too slow for closed-loop control: the released Geo4D checkpoint needs 25 UNet evaluations and 21.8 s per prediction on an RTX 5090. Few-step distillation is the standard remedy for single-view video diffusion, yet it has not been evaluated on a multi-view RGB-D model, and pixel metrics alone cannot tell whether geometry survives. We first show that cutting the Geo4D sampler to one to four steps without training raises PSNR while seed diversity, LPIPS and sharpness degrade, so pixel metrics hide the collapse. We then distill the teacher into a three-step generator with distribution matching distillation in EDM sigma space, sample by re-noising x₀ predictions, and correct a global depth scale shift with a per-view scale anchored to the conditioning pointmap, which needs no ground truth. Two negative results, ODE-regression initialization and a ground-truth-supervised cross-view depth loss, are reported as such. With bf16 inference and the per-view input anchor, the student runs in 1.64 s on one RTX 5090 (13.3x faster than the 21.8 s teacher), its sharpness and seed diversity are statistically indistinguishable from the teacher's, and it loses a small but significant amount of depth accuracy (AbsRel +0.015) and perceptual quality (LPIPS +0.018).

# 1 Introduction

A robot that can predict how a scene will look a few seconds ahead, in 3D and from every camera it has, can plan and verify actions before executing them. Geo4D (Liu et al., 2025) is a recent world model of this kind. Built on Stable Video Diffusion (Blattmann et al., 2023), it takes one RGB-D frame (a color image and a pointmap) per camera view for two views and generates 10 future RGB-D frames for both views, with the pointmaps of both views expressed in the left camera frame and no camera poses required at inference. Trained with a cross-view pointmap alignment loss, it reaches a success rate of 0.53 on the bimanual PlaceAppleFromBowlIntoBin task in the LBM simulator (Toyota Research Institute, 2025), where Dreamitate (Liang et al., 2024), Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) and DP3 (Ze et al., 2024) reach 0.10, 0.00 and 0.00 respectively, as reported by Liu et al. (2025).

The cost of this capability is the sampler. Geo4D generates a latent video with a 25-step EulerEDM sampler (Karras et al., 2022) under classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), and the authors themselves list the resulting inference time, about 30 s for 10 frames on an RTX 4090, as a limitation that rules out closed-loop planning. We measured 21.8 s of pure inference on an RTX 5090, of which the UNet accounts for 20.1 s; the bottleneck is running the same network 25 times, not memory. We set three target budgets: about 2 s for quasi-static pick-and-place, 0.3 to 0.5 s for humanoid control, and 0.1 s for dynamic manipulation. Closing this gap means removing most of the sampling steps.

Few-step sampling is a well-studied problem for image and video diffusion. Progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), consistency models (Song et al., 2023) and distribution matching distillation (DMD; Yin et al., 2024a; Yin et al., 2024b) reduce image generation to one to four network evaluations, and CausVid (Yin et al., 2025), Self-Forcing (Huang et al., 2025) and Causal Forcing (Zhu et al., 2026) extend DMD-style distillation to autoregressive video models. Recent robot world models report 10.8 and 15.3 FPS without evaluating multi-view geometry (DreamDojo, Gao et al., 2026; RoboWorld, Jeon et al., 2026). None of this work asks what happens to a model whose output is not a single video but a pair of pointmap-and-color videos that must agree with each other in 3D. Geo4D has eight latent channels per frame, two views that share all UNet weights and communicate only through the left view's hidden states passed to the right view as spatial context, and a quality criterion, cross-view geometric consistency, that no existing distillation benchmark measures. Whether that criterion survives distillation is the question this paper addresses.

The question cannot be answered with pixel metrics. When we reduced the Geo4D sampler from 25 to 1 Euler step without training (Figure 2), PSNR rose from 19.75 to 20.64 dB while LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018) worsened by 20.7%, Laplacian sharpness fell by 12.5% and the per-pixel standard deviation across four seeds fell by 77%; at one step the moving arm becomes a translucent smear and all four seeds give the same image. This is the perception-distortion tradeoff (Blau & Michaeli, 2018): few-step output approaches the conditional mean, which is blurry but scores well on a pixel average dominated by static background. The metrics also collapse in a fixed order, seed diversity first (from 8 steps), then LPIPS (4 steps), then cross-view geometry (2 steps), and PSNR never. A cross-view chamfer distance is not a substitute either, since it rewards two views that are wrong in the same way. We therefore evaluate every method on per-view depth AbsRel, LPIPS, sharpness, seed diversity and cross-view chamfer jointly, with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (Wilcoxon, 1945) over 40 view-samples, and we report a training-free few-step teacher as a control row in the main comparison.

Our approach is summarized in Figure 1. We distill the frozen Geo4D teacher into a three-step student with DMD (Yin et al., 2024a) rewritten in EDM sigma space, with the backward simulation of DMD2 (Yin et al., 2024b). The student is sampled by predicting x₀ and re-noising to the next sigma rather than by Euler integration, and it uses no classifier-free guidance, so each UNet call processes half the batch of the teacher. A training diagnostic, the ratio of the student's latent standard deviation to the teacher's, tracks the recovery from a foggy initialization to teacher-level sharpness and selects the checkpoint. The student's remaining error is a global depth scale shift, a median of about 9% too far; because Geo4D receives the conditioning pointmap as input, we compute a per-view scale that aligns the predicted first frame to that pointmap at inference time, without ground-truth depth (the right view uses the known camera extrinsics to bring its conditioning pointmap into the reference frame). Finally, we run the UNet, conditioner and VAE decoder in bf16, which the released inference path did not do. Along the way we obtained two negative results that we report as such: initializing the student by regressing teacher ODE trajectories converges to the mean even with true intermediate states, and adding a cross-view depth-ratio loss to DMD (targeting the teacher's ratio in one run and the ground-truth ratio in two) reduced sharpness in all three configurations we tried, and the last one collapsed.

On 20 samples (40 view-samples) of the PlaceAppleFromBowlIntoBin task, the three-step student runs in 1.64 s on one RTX 5090, 13.3x faster than the 21.8 s teacher, and meets the 2 s quasi-static budget (1.18 s with torch.compile, timed on 5 samples). Its sharpness (p=0.95) and seed diversity (p=0.84) are statistically indistinguishable from the teacher's, while AbsRel is higher by 0.015 (p<0.001) and LPIPS by 0.018 (p<0.001). The training-free three-step teacher is also fast (2.81 s in fp32) and keeps the teacher's AbsRel, but loses 47% of its seed diversity, and the one-step student halves diversity and raises LPIPS to 0.177; neither meets the quality bar. The humanoid and dynamic budgets remain open, because even with the compiled decoder 0.63 s of conditioner and decoder cost (0.98 s in the bf16 headline configuration) is independent of the number of steps.

In summary, our contributions are: (1) the first geometry-aware evaluation of few-step distillation for a multi-view RGB-D world model, with a protocol that measures per-view depth accuracy, perceptual quality, sharpness, seed diversity and cross-view consistency jointly and tests each difference against the teacher with paired statistics; (2) a set of evaluation findings and pitfalls: pixel metrics hide few-step collapse, the metrics collapse in a fixed order, a cross-view chamfer distance rewards consistent errors, and on average 53% (35-55% per sample) of the right-view ground-truth pixels produced by the released dataset transform are transformed invalid pixels that distort depth metrics unless masked; (3) an input-anchored per-view depth calibration that requires no ground truth, reduces the student's AbsRel from 0.175 to 0.082 against a teacher value of 0.066, and also improves the teacher; (4) two negative results, ODE-regression initialization and ground-truth-supervised cross-view geometry loss under DMD, together with a three-step student that is 13.3x faster than Geo4D with teacher-level sharpness and diversity.
