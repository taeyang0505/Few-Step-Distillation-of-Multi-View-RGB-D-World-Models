# 5 Limitation

All measurements come from one task (PlaceAppleFromBowlIntoBin), four inference episodes, 20 samples (40 view-samples), and one RTX 5090; the absolute numbers may not transfer to other tasks or to real robot data. The 3-step student is not equal to the teacher: AbsRel is 0.015 higher (p<0.001; 0.022 on the right view) and LPIPS 0.018 higher (p<0.001), and the per-view anchor reduces but does not remove the right-view gap. The 1-step student halves diversity (0.0117) and raises LPIPS to 0.177, so it does not meet our quality bar. Only the quasi-static budget of 2 s is met. The humanoid (0.3-0.5 s) and dynamic (0.1 s) budgets are not, because the conditioner (0.30 s) and VAE decoders (0.33 s) form a fixed cost of 0.63 s that step reduction cannot touch, and the three UNet calls still take 0.55 s; reducing these requires quantization such as SVDQuant (Li et al., 2025) or a lower resolution, which we did not try. We have no policy-level evaluation: Geo4D's closed-loop success rates (Liu et al., 2025, Table 2) require the LBM simulator, a pose tracker, and a policy interface that are not in the released code, so the claim that a faster model enables closed-loop planning remains untested (Appendix A.6). The components we use, DMD (Yin et al., 2024a; 2024b), re-noising few-step sampling, bf16, and torch.compile, are existing techniques; what is new is the geometry-aware measurement and the anchor. Finally, a self-anchor loss that applies the calibration during training is implemented but still training, and we report no results for it.

# 6 Conclusion

We asked whether the geometric consistency of a multi-view RGB-D world model survives few-step distillation. For Geo4D it does, with qualifications. Training-free step reduction keeps PSNR but loses diversity, perceptual quality, and geometry in a fixed order that pixel metrics do not show. Distribution matching distillation restores sharpness and diversity at three steps but shifts the predicted depth by a global scale, which a training-free, input-anchored calibration removes without ground truth. The resulting student produces a two-view, 10-frame RGB-D prediction in 1.64 s, 13.3x faster than the 21.80 s teacher, with AbsRel 0.082 against 0.066 and sharpness and diversity indistinguishable from the teacher. We also report that ODE regression initialization and GT-supervised geometry losses fail, that 53% of right-view ground-truth pixels in the released data are invalid, and that the two views of Geo4D share all UNet weights. We hope these measurements and pitfalls help in building world models that are both fast and geometrically reliable.

# 7 Ethics Statement

This work uses only the simulation data released with Geo4D (LBM, Drake-based); no human subjects, personal data, or real robot trials are involved. Faster video world models could in principle be misused to generate misleading video, but the model studied here produces 10 frames of a 256 × 320 robot manipulation scene from RGB-D input, and the work targets manipulation in simulation. We see no path to harmful use beyond that of the base model.

# 8 Reproducibility Statement

Code, training and evaluation scripts, configuration files, and the qualitative grids are released at https://github.com/taeyang0505/Few-Step-Distillation-of-Multi-View-RGB-D-World-Models. All experiments ran on a single RTX 5090 (32 GB). The teacher checkpoint is the public one released by the Geo4D authors; the student checkpoint corresponds to DMD step 1600. Evaluation uses data seed 1234 and generation seed 0, with seeds 0-3 for diversity. Runtimes: ODE pair generation about 2 h for 284 pairs; ODE regression v2 1200 steps; DMD 2000 steps in 1 h at a peak of 24.4 GB; inference 21.8 s per sample for the teacher and 1.64 s for the student.
